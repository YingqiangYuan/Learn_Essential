## 📋 What You'll Learn

By the end of this section, you'll be able to:

- Understand the core communication principle in American workplaces — async first
- Write messages that people understand the first time they read them

## 🎯 Why This Matters

Picture this: you just started at an American company, and your manager sends you a message on Teams. You read it and reply with "OK."

And then… your manager is completely lost. "OK" meaning what, exactly? You agree with Option A or Option B? You'll do it today or tomorrow?

In American workplaces, a vague "Are you there?" message can instantly brand you as a poor communicator. A well-structured message, on the other hand, makes you look competent and professional on the spot. How you communicate directly shapes how people perceive you — that's not an exaggeration, it's the reality of remote work.

This guide teaches you the most important communication principle in American workplaces: **async first**. Nobody expects you to be online 24/7 firing off instant replies. But every single message you send needs to be **self-contained** — the reader should understand exactly what you need in one pass.

## 📖 Core Concepts

### What Is Async Communication?

Let's clarify two terms first:

**Synchronous communication** = phone calls, video meetings, face-to-face conversations. Both parties must be online at the same time, going back and forth in real time.

**Asynchronous communication** = messages, emails. The other person can reply later — no real-time response needed.

American workplaces default to async. The reason is simple: team members may be spread across time zones, or they're deep in code, or they're in another meeting. Interrupting people on a whim drags the entire team's productivity down.

### When to Use What

| Tool | When to Use | Think of It As… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teams / Slack | Day-to-day communication, quick discussions, file sharing | Your work group chat |
| Outlook / Email | Formal records, contracts, cross-company communication | Official correspondence |
**One rule to remember**: if you can handle it in Teams Chat, don't send an email.

## 🛠️ Essential Teams Operations

### Logging In and Building the Habit

Go to [teams.microsoft.com](http://teams.microsoft.com/) and sign in with your company email.

**Pro tip**: Set Teams as your browser's homepage. That way, every time you open your computer, you're automatically in "work mode" and won't miss important messages.

### How to Write Messages the Right Way

❌ **Don't do this**:

```
"Hey, you there?"
(30 minutes pass...)
"Got a question"
(Another 30 minutes...)
"It's about that project"

```

This drip-feed style of messaging is a disaster in an async environment — the other person might have to wait through several rounds before they even understand what you're asking.

✅ **Do this instead**:

```
Re: tomorrow's project meeting — I've put together three options (see attachment).
I'd recommend Option 2: lowest cost and we can still hit the deadline.
Which one works best for you? I need a decision by 5 PM today so I can reach out to the vendor.

```

**The key**: pack everything into one message — **context + question + what you need back + deadline**. Make your message speak for itself.

## 🔧 Three Techniques You Must Know

### Technique 1: Quote Reply

When a chat has multiple topics going at once, replying with just "OK" leaves the other person guessing — which topic are you responding to?

**How to do it**: Hover your mouse over the specific message you want to reply to, click the Reply icon (a curved arrow) that appears, then type your response in the reply box that pops up.

[Image placeholder: Teams chat interface showing the Reply icon on hover]

This way, the other person instantly knows which topic you're addressing. It's a basic workplace communication skill — make it a habit.

### Technique 2: Emoji Acknowledgments

When you receive a message, a quick 👍 or ✅ reaction is more efficient than typing out "Got it."

**How to do it**: Hover over the message, click the emoji icon (a smiley face) that appears, and select 👍.

It takes one second, but it tells the other person "message received" — so they're not left wondering whether you even saw it.

### Technique 3: @ Mentions

Type `` @ `` followed by a colleague's name in your message to send them a specific notification.

**But use it sparingly!**

An @ mention interrupts whatever the other person is doing. Only use it when something is **urgent** or when you **genuinely need a specific person to respond**. Don't @ a dozen people in every message.

## 🎥 Screen Recording: The Best Way to Show a Problem

Hit a bug? Stuck on how to do something? Instead of spending ages typing out a description, **a 30-second screen recording** lets your mentor understand your problem instantly.

### How to Record Your Screen in Teams

**Step 1: Create a meeting with just yourself**

Click the Calendar icon in the left sidebar, then click the New meeting button in the upper right. Enter something like "[Screen Recording] Demo of XX issue" as the title, **don't invite anyone**, and click Save.

**Step 2: Join and start recording**

Open the meeting you just created and click Join. Once you're in, click the ⋯ (three dots) menu in the upper right and select Start recording. Then click the Share button and choose the window you want to show. When you're done, click ⋯ → Stop recording, then Leave the meeting.

**Step 3: Share the recording**

Wait a few minutes for the system to process the video. Once it's ready, a recording link will appear in the Meeting Chat. Copy that link and send it to your mentor.

## 📱 Teams Mobile App: Don't Miss Meetings

### Why You Absolutely Need It

Let's start with a real workplace landmine: **missing a meeting without a word**.

In American workplaces, being late or having something come up — both totally understandable. But only if **you let people know**. Even a message one minute before the meeting — "Sorry, something came up, I can't make it" — is perfectly fine.

But if you simply don't show up and don't say anything, that's a **no-show** — and it's a serious professional strike. Employees who repeatedly no-show are prime candidates for getting laid off.

The problem is that many people haven't built the habit of checking their calendar regularly. By the time they remember, the meeting is already over.

**The fix is simple**: install the Teams app on your phone.

This isn't about replying to messages around the clock (remember, async — you reply when it's convenient). It's about being able to **check** your meeting schedule and important messages at any time. When you're out and away from your computer, at least you can see "Oh, I have a meeting at 3 PM today" and not miss it entirely.

### Bonus: Multi-Account Switching

The Teams app supports **multiple accounts logged in simultaneously** with one-tap switching.

Think of it as a group chat app that actually lets you run multiple accounts — your school Teams, your company Teams, your personal Outlook email, all managed in one app. Tap your profile picture and switch to whichever account you need. No re-entering passwords every time.

**How to set it up**: Search for Microsoft Teams in your phone's app store, download it, and sign in with your company email. Once you're in, tap your profile picture in the upper left → Add account, and add your other email accounts. After that, switching accounts is just a tap on your profile picture and selecting the one you want.

## ⭐ The Golden Rules of Workplace Communication

Remember this mantra: **"Close every loop: acknowledge, follow through, and report back."**

| Principle | What It Means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acknowledge everything | State the context, requirements, and timeline clearly | "Here's a first draft of the PPT for next week. Could you check whether the logic flows? Feedback by end of day tomorrow works." |
| Follow through reliably | Confirm receipt promptly; proactively report progress | React with 👍 when you get a task; speak up immediately if you hit a snag |
| Always close the loop | Let people know when you're done | Report back when it's finished; give a heads-up early if it won't be |

## 👨‍🏫 A Note from Your Mentor: Communication Is Your Brand

Now that you've learned these techniques, you might be thinking: "It's just sending messages — does it really need to be this involved?"

Let me share a deeper insight: **In the remote work era, your professional image is shaped almost entirely through communication tools.**

### Your Messages Are Your Identity

Think about it. In a remote environment, your colleagues can't see how you dress, can't hear your tone of voice, and have no idea whether you're working hard at your desk. Their entire impression of you comes from one thing — every message you send.

A well-structured, complete message signals: "This person thinks clearly, delivers reliably, and can be trusted."

A vague "Hey, you there?" signals: "This person isn't great at communicating — they might need a lot of hand-holding."

That's why I say **how you communicate is your professional brand**.

### The Deeper Value of Async Thinking

"Async first" isn't just a work habit — it reflects a deeper mindset of **respecting other people's time**.

When you send a self-contained message, what you're really saying is: "I respect your time. I don't want to derail your work with inefficient back-and-forth. I've organized all the information so you can understand everything at a glance, whenever it's convenient for you."

This mindset makes you welcome on any team. Because everyone prefers working with someone who makes their life easier.

### Three Habits to Start Building Today

Here are my three recommendations:

1. **Before you send a message, ask yourself**: "If the other person doesn't see this for three hours, will they understand exactly what I need in one read?" If the answer is no, reorganize it.
2. **When you receive a message, signal immediately**. Even just a 👍 is better than silence. Let the other person know "got it — I'll take care of it."
3. **When you finish a task, proactively report back**. Don't wait for someone to chase you with "Is it done yet?" — take the initiative and give them peace of mind.

These three habits sound simple, but surprisingly few people stick with them. And that's exactly what separates "reliable" from "average."
