# Teams Communication Rules: All Project Talk Happens on Company Channels

## 📋 What You'll Learn

By the end of this section, you'll be able to:

- Understand why all project communication must happen on Teams, not personal messaging apps
- Install and sign in to Microsoft Teams on both your computer and phone
- Know exactly what belongs on Teams and what can stay on personal channels

## 🎯 Why Are We Setting This Rule?

Picture this: you're discussing a project task with your mentor over iMessage. A few days later, you get a new phone — and poof, that conversation is gone. Your mentor tries to find the discussion from three weeks ago and can't track it down. Even worse, they sent you detailed feedback on a technical question, but it got buried under a pile of new messages and you never saw it.

This kind of thing happens all the time with personal messaging apps. iMessage, Discord, GroupMe — these tools are built for hanging out with friends, not for getting work done. Chat history is unreliable, search is terrible, messages get lost when you switch devices, and file sharing is an afterthought.

Microsoft Teams is built for work. Every message is permanently saved, fully searchable, synced across all your devices in real time, and file management actually works. More importantly, it's the industry standard — nearly every company in the US runs on Teams or something similar (Slack, Google Chat).

**So starting today, here's the rule: all communication related to the project, coursework, or tasks happens on Teams.**

This isn't about making your life harder. It's about working the way professionals work, from day one.

---

## 📖 The Core Rule: What Goes on Teams, What Doesn't

Simple. One principle:

**Project-related → Teams. Everything else → use whatever you want.**

Here's what that looks like in practice:

**Must happen on Teams:**

- Questions and discussions about course content
- Anything related to task cards
- Sharing code, documents, and files
- Project progress updates
- Feedback and guidance from your mentor

**Personal channels (iMessage, Discord, etc.) are only for:**

- Urgent alerts (like "today's meeting is cancelled" — time-sensitive stuff)
- Critical schedule changes that can't wait

**If you ask a project question on a personal channel, your mentor will redirect you to Teams.** That's not them being difficult — it's building a good habit. When discussions happen on Teams, everything is recorded, searchable, and traceable. That's helpful for you too when you want to review something later.

## 📖 Why Company Tools Beat Personal Apps

You might be thinking: "I can chat on iMessage just fine, why do I need Teams?"

Let's look at it from a few angles:

**History and Search**

- Personal apps: Chat history can vanish when you switch phones, search is weak, group chats get buried under new messages
- Teams: Every message is permanently stored in the cloud, fully searchable — find a discussion from three months ago in seconds

**Multi-device Sync**

- Personal apps: Switching devices means manually migrating chat history (if it even works), and you might lose messages in the process
- Teams: Phone, laptop, web browser — all synced in real time. Sign in on any device and your entire history is right there

**File Management**

- Personal apps: Files expire and become undownloadable, large files won't send at all
- Teams: Files are stored long-term, supports large files, and you can even edit Office documents directly in the app

**Professionalism**

- Personal apps: Social life and work life mixed together — constant distractions from memes and group chats
- Teams: Purpose-built for work, with channels that keep conversations organized and focused

In the American workplace, nobody discusses work on iMessage or Discord. All official communication happens on company tools. Building this habit now means you'll hit the ground running at your first job.

## 🛠️ Setting Up Teams: Computer + Phone

### On Your Computer

Go to [teams.microsoft.com](https://teams.microsoft.com) and sign in with your company email (the one your organization gave you).

**Pro tip**: Set Teams as your browser's homepage. That way, every time you open your computer, you're automatically in "work mode" and won't miss important messages.

### On Your Phone (Required!)

Search for **Microsoft Teams** in the App Store (iPhone) or Google Play Store (Android), download it, and sign in with the same company email.

Why do you need it on your phone too? Two reasons:

**First, never be a no-show.** In the American workplace, being late or having something come up is totally understandable — but only if **you let people know**. Even a message one minute before the meeting — "Sorry, something came up, I can't make it" — is perfectly fine. But if you simply don't show up and don't say anything, that's a **no-show**, and it's a serious professional strike. Having Teams on your phone means you can always see what's on your calendar, so you never miss a meeting without warning.

**Second, stay in the loop.** This isn't about replying instantly (reply when it's convenient — that's what async means). It's about being able to **see** whether anything urgent came up, even when you're away from your computer.

### Multi-Account Switching

The Teams app supports **multiple accounts logged in at the same time** with one-tap switching. Think of it like having separate inboxes for school, work, and personal — all in one app. No re-entering passwords every time.

**How to set it up**: After signing in, tap your profile picture in the top-left → Add account, and add your other email accounts. Switching between them is just a tap on your profile picture.

## 📖 How Our Group Chats Are Organized

We create a Teams group chat for each student. This group chat is your **primary communication hub** with your mentor — all course discussions, Q&A, and progress updates happen here.

Why a group chat instead of one-on-one DMs? Because a group chat can include multiple mentors and teaching assistants. When one mentor is unavailable, others can still see your question and jump in to help.

**After you receive the group chat invite, do these three things:**

1. Confirm you can open the group chat on both your computer and phone
2. Send a quick message to say hello and verify everything works
3. From now on, bring all project-related questions here

## 👨‍🏫 Mentor's Note: The Workplace Logic Behind This Rule

You might be thinking "does it really matter which chat app I use?" But there's a deeper workplace logic behind this rule.

### Professional Tools = Professional Mindset

When you open iMessage or Discord, you see social feeds, meme channels, and friend group chats — your brain automatically shifts into "social mode." But when you open Teams, everything on screen is work-related — your brain shifts into "work mode."

Tools shape mindset. People who communicate through professional tools tend to write higher-quality messages — because the environment itself reminds you: "this is work, take it seriously."

### This Is How Real Companies Operate

At every real company, all work communication happens on company tools. This isn't just about convenience — it's about **compliance and information security**. Companies need to ensure that work discussions are documented, files don't get lost, and sensitive information doesn't leak into personal channels.

Building this habit now means you'll transition seamlessly into any company. You won't be the person asking "can I just text you instead?" — you'll be the professional who communicates clearly on Slack or Teams from day one.

### It Benefits You Too

When all discussions live on Teams, you can search for anything at any time. Want to revisit a discussion about a concept from three months ago? Just search for it. That's something your personal chat history can't do reliably.

**Think of Teams as your work journal — every valuable discussion stays here, always accessible when you need it.**
